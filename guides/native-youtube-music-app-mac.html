What makes a Mac app feel native?

You notice a native Mac app not by its code, but by how it behaves. It fits in with Finder, Mail and Apple Music: it has its own window and Dock icon, follows the Mac's shortcuts and works with the system – media keys, Control Center, full screen. A web page in a browser tab does none of that, and a wrapped website often only half of it.

How Tubemac makes YouTube Music feel native

  • A real Mac window. No address bar, no title strip. Artist photos and album colors flow to the top edge, and the window buttons sit neatly in line with the toolbar. Drag the toolbar to move the window, double-click it to zoom.
  • Built into macOS. Play, pause and skip with the media keys, AirPods or Touch Bar – from any app. Control Center shows one clean card with artwork, back, play/pause, next and a slider.
  • Behaves like a music app. Closing the window keeps the music playing; click the Dock icon to bring it back. The search sits centered in the toolbar, and the interface stays pinned instead of bouncing.
  • Small and efficient. Written in Swift on Apple's own web engine – the one Safari uses – so it's just 1 MB and runs natively on Apple Silicon and Intel.
  • Nothing missing. Inside runs the official YouTube Music, so your library, playlists, lyrics, Premium and every new feature work immediately – with free accounts too.

What about other YouTube Music apps?

Kaset rebuilds the whole YouTube Music interface in Apple's SwiftUI. It's native through and through, but requires macOS 15.4 and a Premium subscription for full playback, and new YouTube Music features only arrive with app updates. YTMDesktop and Pear Desktop are popular cross-platform apps built with Electron: they bundle their own Chrome (about 118 MB on Mac) and look more like a Chrome window than a Mac app. A full comparison is in 5 ways to get YouTube Music on Mac.

Try Tubemac in two minutes

  1. Download Tubemac (1 MB) from the Tubemac website and double-click the zip file.
  2. Drag Tubemac into your Applications folder and open it.
  3. If macOS asks, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway (why?).
  4. Click Sign in and log in with your Google account – Tubemac remembers you from then on.

FAQ

Is there a native YouTube Music app for Mac?

Not from Google – YouTube Music officially only runs in the browser on macOS. Tubemac is a free Mac app (1 MB, Swift, no Electron) that makes YouTube Music feel like a native Mac app, with an edge-to-edge window, Control Center, media keys and background playback.

Does Tubemac feel like a native Mac app?

Yes, that's what it's built for: its own window with the window buttons in the toolbar, artwork running edge to edge, Mac menus and ⌘ shortcuts, media keys, a clean Control Center card and music that keeps playing when the window is closed.

Is Tubemac an Electron app?

No. Tubemac is written in Swift and uses Apple's WebKit, the engine behind Safari. That's why it's only about 1 MB, while Electron-based YouTube Music apps are around 118 MB.

Is there a native-feeling YouTube Music app for Mac that works without Premium?

Yes. Tubemac works with free and Premium accounts, because it shows the official YouTube Music player.
