Short answer: macOS blocks apps from outside the App Store that aren't notarized by Apple – many free apps, including YouTube Music apps like Tubemac and Kaset, aren't. Try to open the app once, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway. You only need to do this once per app.

Why macOS shows this warning

Apple's Gatekeeper checks every app you download. Apps that are signed and “notarized” with a paid Apple developer membership open straight away. Free hobby projects often skip that membership, so macOS shows a warning such as “Apple could not verify ‘Tubemac’ is free of malware” or “‘Tubemac’ Not Opened”. It doesn't mean the app is broken – it means macOS wants you to confirm that you trust it.

macOS 15 Sequoia and macOS 26 Tahoe

  1. Move the app into your Applications folder and double-click it. When the warning appears, click Done.
  2. Open System Settings and choose Privacy & Security in the sidebar.
  3. Scroll down to Security. You'll see a message that the app was blocked – click Open Anyway.
  4. Confirm with your password or Touch ID, then click Open Anyway in the dialog that appears.

From now on the app opens normally, like any other app.

macOS 14 Sonoma

  1. In the Applications folder, hold Control and click the app (or right-click it).
  2. Choose Open, then click Open in the dialog.

The Open Anyway button in System Settings → Privacy & Security works on Sonoma too.

For advanced users: Terminal

Removing the “downloaded from the internet” flag has the same effect:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Tubemac.app

Replace the app name with the one you installed.

How to stay safe

  • Download only from the official source – the project's own website or its GitHub releases page, not from download portals.
  • Prefer open-source apps whose code anyone can read. Tubemac, Kaset, YTMDesktop and Pear Desktop are all open source.
  • Never turn off Gatekeeper completely – allow individual apps instead.

FAQ

Why does my Mac say Tubemac can't be opened?

Tubemac is a free app that isn't notarized by Apple, so macOS asks you to confirm it once. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.

Where is “Open Anyway” on macOS Sequoia?

In System Settings → Privacy & Security, in the Security section further down. It only appears after you have tried to open the app once.

Is it safe to click Open Anyway?

It's safe for apps you trust and downloaded from their official source. Open-source apps like Tubemac publish their full code, so anyone can check what they do.
